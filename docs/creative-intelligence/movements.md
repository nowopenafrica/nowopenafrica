These are public design history in our own words. Nothing here describes or copies a specific design.

<a id="swiss-international"></a>
## Swiss International

Mid-century Swiss and German schools: objective communication on a mathematical grid.

**Principles**
- The grid is visible in the result
- Asymmetric balance, never centred by default
- One type family, contrast from size and weight only
- Photography as evidence, not decoration

- **Grid:** Strict column grid, 8 to 12 columns, flush-left ragged-right, consistent baseline.
- **Typography:** A neutral sans in two weights. Large size jumps (3x or more) between levels. No italics for emphasis.
- **Colour:** Black, white and one signal colour used on 10% of the surface or less.
- **Imagery:** none, framed, full-bleed
- **Compositions:** Modular grid; Typographic poster; Editorial split
- **Pairings we ship:** swiss, modern, system
- **Palette families:** mono, cool, bold (sample: Paper & Ink)

**Use when**
- The content is information: dates, programmes, specifications
- The client must look rigorous and international
- There is little or no imagery to rely on

**Avoid when**
- The brand sells warmth, indulgence or celebration
- The audience expects ornament (weddings, festive retail)
- There is one short line and nothing else to organise

**Pitfalls**
- Centred text with a grid drawn around it
- Three or more colours
- Body copy set too small to honour the white space

<a id="bauhaus"></a>
## Bauhaus

1920s German school: form follows function, built from circle, square and triangle.

**Principles**
- Geometry is the vocabulary
- Primary colours carry meaning
- Type is constructed, lower-case and functional
- Nothing is added that does no work

- **Grid:** Rectilinear blocks with one diagonal or circular counter-move.
- **Typography:** Geometric sans, often all lower-case, set in blocks that align to the shapes.
- **Colour:** Red, yellow and blue with black and off-white. Flat, no gradients.
- **Imagery:** none, illustration, cut-out
- **Compositions:** Diagonal energy; Modular grid; Typographic poster
- **Pairings we ship:** bauhaus, modern, statement, city
- **Palette families:** bold, mono (sample: Sun & Ink)

**Use when**
- Schools, workshops, architecture, design and making
- A playful rigour is wanted: serious but not stiff
- The piece must work with no photograph

**Avoid when**
- Luxury or heritage positioning
- Food that must look appetising
- More than about forty words of copy

**Pitfalls**
- Random shapes scattered as decoration
- Using all three primaries at equal weight
- Soft shadows or gradients

<a id="luxury-minimalism"></a>
## Luxury Minimalism

Fashion, fragrance and property: value shown by what is left out.

**Principles**
- One focal object or line per surface
- Generous margins are the main signal of value
- Material cues over graphic effects: paper, foil, stone
- Small type, widely tracked, perfectly spaced

- **Grid:** Centred or strict two-column, margins of 12% or more of the short edge.
- **Typography:** High-contrast serif or refined sans. Capitals tracked +8 to +20%. Three sizes at most.
- **Colour:** Two neutrals and one metal or deep tone. No saturated colour except a product.
- **Imagery:** framed, cut-out, full-bleed, none
- **Compositions:** Centred emblem; Mounted frame; Product hero
- **Pairings we ship:** vogue, couture, luxury, atelier, monument
- **Palette families:** mono, earth (sample: Baobab)

**Use when**
- Skincare, fragrance, jewellery, premium property, hotels
- The product photograph is excellent
- Price is high and the buyer is unhurried

**Avoid when**
- A sale, discount or urgent offer
- The only image available is weak: emptiness will expose it
- Mass-market or price-led retail

**Pitfalls**
- Empty space with nothing excellent inside it
- Fake gold gradients
- Script fonts standing in for elegance

<a id="neo-brutalism"></a>
## Neo-Brutalism

A web-born reaction to polish: raw structure, hard edges, visible joints.

**Principles**
- Show the structure: boxes, borders, labels
- Hard shadows and thick rules, never soft
- Clashing colour used with confidence
- Type at uncomfortable sizes

- **Grid:** Stacked boxes with heavy outlines; deliberate overlap and offset.
- **Typography:** Heavy grotesque or mono, tight leading, labels in capitals.
- **Colour:** Black outlines over one or two loud flats; white as a colour.
- **Imagery:** none, cut-out, duotone
- **Compositions:** Monumental type; Band and field; Layered collage
- **Pairings we ship:** impact, workshop, statement, street, broadcast, works
- **Palette families:** bold, mono (sample: Electric Lagos)

**Use when**
- Youth, music, streetwear, tech products with attitude
- The message is short and blunt
- Standing out matters more than looking established

**Avoid when**
- Health, finance, law or anything that must reassure
- Older or conservative audiences
- Long copy

**Pitfalls**
- Raw without rigour: misaligned boxes read as mistakes
- Low-contrast colour clashes that hurt legibility
- Using it for a brand that is actually gentle

<a id="editorial"></a>
## Editorial

Magazine art direction: the picture and the headline argue, and the page is the idea.

**Principles**
- Image-led storytelling with a strong crop
- A headline with a voice, not a label
- Deliberate scale contrast between display and text
- White space paces the reading

- **Grid:** Asymmetric multi-column with a clear dominant area of 60% or more.
- **Typography:** Display serif against a quiet sans; small capitals for labels; real typographic marks.
- **Colour:** Drawn from the photograph, plus black and paper.
- **Imagery:** full-bleed, framed, cut-out
- **Compositions:** Editorial split; Image to the edges; Mounted frame
- **Pairings we ship:** gallery, vogue, editorial, couture, atelier, masthead
- **Palette families:** mono, earth, warm (sample: Terracotta Clay)

**Use when**
- Fashion, culture, restaurants, hospitality, profiles of people
- There is a photograph worth building a page around
- The audience will give it more than two seconds

**Avoid when**
- No strong image exists
- Outdoor at speed, where subtlety is lost
- Price lists and dense practical information

**Pitfalls**
- A weak stock photo treated as a hero
- Headline that only names the category
- Too many small text blocks

<a id="contemporary-maximalism"></a>
## Contemporary Maximalism

More is more, under control: layered pattern, colour and type with one clear path through.

**Principles**
- Density with hierarchy: one element still wins
- Pattern on pattern, tied by a limited palette
- Mixed type voices, each with a job
- Energy from overlap and repetition

- **Grid:** Layered, with a hidden grid holding the edges and the reading line.
- **Typography:** Two or three contrasting faces; one very large; outlines and stacks allowed.
- **Colour:** Four to six saturated colours from one family logic, anchored by black or white.
- **Imagery:** collage, cut-out, pattern, illustration
- **Compositions:** Layered collage; Pattern field; Monumental type
- **Pairings we ship:** impact, street, statement, masthead, playroom
- **Palette families:** bold, warm (sample: Carnival)

**Use when**
- Festivals, launches, youth retail, food with personality
- The brand is loud by nature
- There are many assets to combine

**Avoid when**
- Premium restraint is the positioning
- Small formats where layers turn to mud
- Anything read in under three seconds

**Pitfalls**
- No single focal point
- Colours chosen at random
- Text placed over busy areas without a panel

<a id="afro-contemporary"></a>
## Afro-Contemporary

Current African visual culture: fashion photography, textile logic and city energy on a modern grid.

**Principles**
- People first: confident portraiture, real skin tones, natural light
- Colour and pattern taken from a specific source, named, not generic "tribal"
- Modern type and grid; heritage in the content, not in clip-art
- Place is specific: a named city, street or cloth

- **Grid:** Modern asymmetric grid; pattern used as a field or edge, never as wallpaper behind text.
- **Typography:** Contemporary grotesque or sharp serif. No faux-ethnic lettering.
- **Colour:** Saturated but ordered: two strong colours and a neutral, taken from the cloth or scene.
- **Imagery:** full-bleed, framed, pattern, cut-out
- **Compositions:** Image to the edges; Framed portrait; Pattern field
- **Pairings we ship:** modern, couture, statement, studio, neighbourhood
- **Palette families:** warm, bold, earth (sample: Kente Field)

**Use when**
- Fashion, beauty, music, hospitality and culture rooted in an African city
- The brand wants to look current and local at once
- Real photography of real people is available

**Avoid when**
- The only "African" element would be a generic pattern
- The pattern belongs to a culture the business has no tie to
- A sober institutional message

**Pitfalls**
- Stereotype: animal prints, maps and masks as shorthand
- Sacred or restricted motifs used as decoration
- Duotones over faces that erase skin tone

<a id="african-modernism"></a>
## African Modernism

Post-independence design and architecture: international modernism rebuilt with local material, climate and craft.

**Principles**
- Geometry from architecture: screens, breeze blocks, rhythm
- Earth and mineral colour with one strong accent
- Craft texture inside a rational structure
- Confidence without nostalgia

- **Grid:** Modular, with repeated units forming screens or bands.
- **Typography:** Rational sans or sturdy serif; captions set with care; numerals prominent.
- **Colour:** Laterite, indigo, ochre, concrete grey; one bright accent.
- **Imagery:** pattern, framed, illustration
- **Compositions:** Pattern field; Modular grid; Band and field
- **Pairings we ship:** swiss, heirloom, gallery, modern, city, studio
- **Palette families:** earth, warm, cool (sample: Adire & Clay)

**Use when**
- Institutions, architecture, property, culture and hospitality
- A brand that wants depth and permanence
- Print where texture can be felt

**Avoid when**
- Fast youth retail
- Brands that need to look brand-new and digital
- Very small digital formats

**Pitfalls**
- Pattern with no structural logic
- Muddy palette with no accent
- Treating it as "retro"

<a id="retro-futurism"></a>
## Retro-Futurism

Yesterday's idea of tomorrow: space-age optimism, technical diagrams, chrome and grain.

**Principles**
- A technical, schematic layer: lines, coordinates, small labels
- One heroic object or shape
- Grain or print texture over clean geometry
- Optimism, not dystopia

- **Grid:** Poster grid with a dominant central or diagonal object and marginal data.
- **Typography:** Wide or condensed display with mono captions; numerals and codes as texture.
- **Colour:** Dark ground with one hot accent, or faded print tones.
- **Imagery:** illustration, render, cut-out, duotone
- **Compositions:** Typographic poster; Rendered stage; Diagonal energy
- **Pairings we ship:** street, modern, poster, signal
- **Palette families:** cool, bold, mono (sample: Midnight Silk)

**Use when**
- Tech events, music, gaming, product launches
- A brand that wants wonder rather than polish
- Posters and covers

**Avoid when**
- Food, health and family services
- When the data labels would be fake: no invented specifications
- Conservative corporate audiences

**Pitfalls**
- Meaningless technical labels
- Neon overload
- Illegible display type

<a id="corporate-precision"></a>
## Corporate Precision

Identity systems for institutions: consistency, clarity and trust across every application.

**Principles**
- A system, not a one-off: rules that repeat
- Clear information order
- Restrained colour with strict roles
- Nothing ambiguous

- **Grid:** Modular grid with fixed zones for mark, title, body and legal.
- **Typography:** One sans family, three weights, strict sizes. Sentence case.
- **Colour:** One brand colour, one support, neutrals. Colour means something each time.
- **Imagery:** framed, none, illustration
- **Compositions:** Band and field; Modular grid; Information ledger
- **Pairings we ship:** swiss, ledger, modern, system, city
- **Palette families:** cool, mono (sample: Harbour Blue)

**Use when**
- Finance, law, consulting, government, B2B technology
- Documents and presentations that many people will extend
- Trust and competence are the message

**Avoid when**
- Entertainment and lifestyle
- A launch that needs excitement
- A small business that should feel personal

**Pitfalls**
- Blue-and-grey anonymity: no distinguishing device
- Stock handshake photographs
- Decorative gradients

<a id="analog-experimental"></a>
## Analog Experimental

The sketchbook and the photocopier: hand-made marks, tape, stencil and misregistration.

**Principles**
- Evidence of the hand: tears, tape, scans
- Imperfection that is clearly chosen
- Type as image: stencil, stamp, cut letters
- Layers with real texture

- **Grid:** Loose, with a firm anchor so the reading line survives.
- **Typography:** Stencil, typewriter or cut-out capitals against a plain reading face.
- **Colour:** Paper tones, black, and one or two spot colours as if printed.
- **Imagery:** collage, duotone, cut-out, illustration
- **Compositions:** Layered collage; Typographic poster; Monumental type
- **Pairings we ship:** street, editorial, poster, studio
- **Palette families:** mono, warm, earth (sample: Red Earth)

**Use when**
- Music, art, zines, independent fashion, cultural events
- The brand values craft and attitude over polish
- Short runs and posters

**Avoid when**
- Health, finance, property
- Where legibility of details is critical
- Clients who will read roughness as carelessness

**Pitfalls**
- Fake texture pasted over a clean layout
- Unreadable essentials
- Every element distressed equally

<a id="cgi-3d"></a>
## 3D and CGI

Rendered objects and materials: impossible products, lit like a studio.

**Principles**
- One hero object with believable material and light
- Type lives in the same light: shadow, reflection, depth
- A simple stage
- Scale play: tiny or monumental

- **Grid:** Central or rule-of-thirds stage; type on a clear plane.
- **Typography:** Simple, confident sans; the render does the talking.
- **Colour:** Set by material and light; one dominant hue.
- **Imagery:** render
- **Compositions:** Rendered stage; Product hero; Monumental type
- **Pairings we ship:** modern, statement, signal
- **Palette families:** warm, cool, mono (sample: Cola Nut)

**Use when**
- Product launches, technology, drinks, sneakers, events
- No photograph of the real thing can exist yet
- Large outdoor and digital screens

**Avoid when**
- A real, good photograph of the product exists
- Food, where renders look false
- When the render would misrepresent what is sold

**Pitfalls**
- Plastic-looking materials
- A render that shows a product the customer will not receive
- Type pasted flat over a lit scene

<a id="art-deco"></a>
## Art Deco

1920s and 30s glamour: symmetry, stepped forms and metallic line.

**Principles**
- Strict symmetry
- Stepped, fanned and sunburst geometry
- Fine line work as ornament
- Capitals with stately spacing

- **Grid:** Central axis with mirrored ornament and framed panels.
- **Typography:** Geometric capitals, tall and widely tracked; a refined serif for text.
- **Colour:** Black, cream and a metal; or deep jewel tones with gold line.
- **Imagery:** illustration, none, framed
- **Compositions:** Centred emblem; Pattern field; Mounted frame
- **Pairings we ship:** deco, luxury, couture, monument
- **Palette families:** earth, mono, cool (sample: Forest & Brass)

**Use when**
- Hotels, bars, jewellery, galas, invitations
- Evening and celebration
- Print with foil or emboss

**Avoid when**
- Technology and startups
- Everyday retail
- Anything that must feel informal

**Pitfalls**
- Ornament crowding the message
- Fake metallic gradients on screen
- Mixing with casual type

<a id="japanese-minimalism"></a>
## Japanese Minimalism

Ma, the active interval: asymmetry, restraint and natural material.

**Principles**
- Empty space is the subject
- Asymmetric placement with exact balance
- One small, precise element
- Natural textures and muted tone

- **Grid:** Off-centre placement on thirds; wide uneven margins.
- **Typography:** Small, light, carefully spaced. One size where possible.
- **Colour:** Paper, ink and one muted natural colour.
- **Imagery:** framed, cut-out, none
- **Compositions:** Mounted frame; Product hero; Centred emblem
- **Pairings we ship:** gallery, couture, atelier
- **Palette families:** mono, earth, pastel (sample: Moss & Stone)

**Use when**
- Wellness, tea and coffee, ceramics, architecture, skincare
- A single object of real quality
- Calm is the promise

**Avoid when**
- Offers and urgency
- Crowded information
- Outdoor at distance

**Pitfalls**
- Borrowed scripts or motifs used as decoration
- Type too small to read
- Emptiness without precision

<a id="y2k-psychedelic"></a>
## Y2K and Psychedelic

Late-90s rave and 60s poster art meet: liquid forms, chrome, warped type.

**Principles**
- Liquid, bulging and warped shapes
- Colour that vibrates on purpose
- Type bent into the image
- Nostalgia with a wink

- **Grid:** Organic, held by a central mass and a clear caption zone.
- **Typography:** Distorted or rounded display; a plain face for the facts.
- **Colour:** Acid brights, gradients and iridescence against black or white.
- **Imagery:** illustration, render, collage
- **Compositions:** Typographic poster; Rendered stage; Layered collage
- **Pairings we ship:** street, impact, signal, artline
- **Palette families:** bold, pastel (sample: Carnival)

**Use when**
- Parties, music, youth fashion, drinks
- Short-lived campaigns
- Audiences under 30

**Avoid when**
- Anything meant to last years
- Trust-led services
- Dense information

**Pitfalls**
- Facts lost in the distortion
- Dated by next season
- Low contrast between vibrating colours and text

<a id="organic-natural"></a>
## Organic Natural

Wellness and food culture: soft form, natural material and honest daylight.

**Principles**
- Soft, irregular shapes from nature
- Daylight photography with real texture
- Earth and plant colour
- Calm, friendly voice

- **Grid:** Relaxed, generous, with rounded containers.
- **Typography:** Soft serif or rounded sans; lower-case warmth; a touch of script for one word.
- **Colour:** Clay, sage, oat, olive; one fruit or flower accent.
- **Imagery:** full-bleed, framed, cut-out, illustration
- **Compositions:** Image to the edges; Product hero; Mounted frame
- **Pairings we ship:** warmth, craft, gallery, neighbourhood
- **Palette families:** earth, pastel, warm (sample: Blush & Sage)

**Use when**
- Skincare, wellness, organic food, farms, cafes, baby products
- Ingredients and origin are the story
- A gentle, trusting audience

**Avoid when**
- Technology and finance
- Nightlife
- Claims that sound medical: check regulation first

**Pitfalls**
- Beige on beige with no contrast
- Generic leaf icons
- "Natural" claims the product cannot support

<a id="kinetic-typography"></a>
## Kinetic Typography

Type designed to move, and stills that look as if they are about to.

**Principles**
- Repetition, stretch and stagger imply motion
- One word or phrase carries everything
- Rhythm over ornament
- Works as a still and as a loop

- **Grid:** Stacked lines with systematic offset or scale steps.
- **Typography:** One bold face repeated; variable width or weight if available.
- **Colour:** Two colours, high contrast.
- **Imagery:** none, duotone
- **Compositions:** Monumental type; Typographic poster; Serial tiles
- **Pairings we ship:** impact, statement, poster, broadcast, works
- **Palette families:** bold, mono (sample: Cobalt Pop)

**Use when**
- Reels covers, event countdowns, sale announcements, sport
- Screens where it can actually move
- A phrase strong enough to stand alone

**Avoid when**
- More than one message
- Detail-heavy pieces
- Print-only luxury

**Pitfalls**
- Repetition with no rhythm
- Stretching type until letterforms break
- Forgetting the practical details entirely

<a id="memphis"></a>
## Memphis

1980s Milan: squiggles, terrazzo and deliberate bad manners.

**Principles**
- Flat shapes and patterns in conversation
- Clashing pastel and primary
- Asymmetry and humour
- Black line holds it together

- **Grid:** Scattered shapes around a stable text block.
- **Typography:** Friendly geometric sans; occasional outline.
- **Colour:** Pastels with one or two primaries and black.
- **Imagery:** illustration, pattern, cut-out
- **Compositions:** Pattern field; Layered collage; Band and field
- **Pairings we ship:** bauhaus, modern, warmth, playroom
- **Palette families:** pastel, bold (sample: Lilac & Mint)

**Use when**
- Children, cafes, stationery, community events, creative studios
- Lightness and humour
- Social posts in series

**Avoid when**
- Serious or premium subjects
- Formal invitations
- When the audience will read it as childish

**Pitfalls**
- Shapes with no relation to each other
- Text floating among shapes with no anchor
- Too many patterns at once

<a id="constructivism"></a>
## Constructivism

1920s Russian avant-garde: diagonal force, photomontage and type as a call to act.

**Principles**
- Strong diagonals
- Two or three flat colours
- Photomontage of people in action
- Type as a shout, built from bars

- **Grid:** Diagonal axis crossing a rigid frame.
- **Typography:** Heavy condensed capitals on bars and wedges.
- **Colour:** Red, black and paper; occasionally one more.
- **Imagery:** duotone, cut-out, collage
- **Compositions:** Diagonal energy; Layered collage; Monumental type
- **Pairings we ship:** poster, workshop, street, impact, works, broadcast
- **Palette families:** bold, mono (sample: Poppy Field)

**Use when**
- Campaigns, rallies, sport, gyms, protest-toned launches
- A call to action is the whole point
- Posters

**Avoid when**
- Gentle or premium brands
- Hospitality
- Where political overtones would be unwelcome

**Pitfalls**
- Diagonals with no reason
- Pastiche of historical propaganda
- Unreadable angled body copy

<a id="heritage-craft"></a>
## Heritage Craft

Makers' marks, engraving and letterpress: trust built from age and skill.

**Principles**
- Emblems, seals and borders with real construction
- Engraved or woodcut illustration
- Dates, places and names stated plainly
- Paper and ink qualities

- **Grid:** Centred label logic with framed panels.
- **Typography:** Old-style serif, small capitals, numerals; script used once.
- **Colour:** One or two inks on warm paper.
- **Imagery:** illustration, none, framed
- **Compositions:** Centred emblem; Information ledger; Pattern field
- **Pairings we ship:** heirloom, craft, workshop, luxury, monument
- **Palette families:** earth, mono, warm (sample: Harmattan Sand)

**Use when**
- Food and drink labels, tailoring, leather, barbers, family businesses
- Provenance is real and can be stated
- Packaging and print

**Avoid when**
- A business with no history to state: never invent "Est." dates
- Technology
- Youth nightlife

**Pitfalls**
- Invented founding dates or awards
- Ornament overload
- Faux-vintage filters

<a id="information-design"></a>
## Information Design

Timetables, maps and reports: making facts quick to find and hard to misread.

**Principles**
- The reader's question decides the order
- Tables, rules and alignment do the work
- Numbers are typeset properly
- Colour encodes, never decorates

- **Grid:** Tabular grid with aligned columns and consistent row rhythm.
- **Typography:** Sans with tabular figures; clear weight steps; generous line height.
- **Colour:** Neutral base; one or two colours with fixed meanings.
- **Imagery:** none, illustration
- **Compositions:** Information ledger; Modular grid; Serial tiles
- **Pairings we ship:** ledger, swiss, system
- **Palette families:** mono, cool (sample: Glacier)

**Use when**
- Menus, price lists, schedules, programmes, reports, service sheets
- Many facts must be compared
- Accuracy matters more than mood

**Avoid when**
- Brand campaigns and single-message posters
- When there are fewer than five facts
- Emotional launches

**Pitfalls**
- Decoration between the reader and the number
- Misaligned currency and decimals
- Colour with no legend

<a id="monochrome-photographic"></a>
## Monochrome Photographic

Black-and-white reportage and fashion: light, grain and a single line of type.

**Principles**
- One strong black-and-white photograph
- Tonal range is the palette
- Minimal type, exactly placed
- Grain as texture

- **Grid:** Full-bleed image with type on a strict margin.
- **Typography:** One refined face, small or very large, never medium.
- **Colour:** Black, white, greys; at most one spot colour.
- **Imagery:** full-bleed, framed
- **Compositions:** Image to the edges; Mounted frame; Editorial split
- **Pairings we ship:** vogue, couture, gallery, swiss, artline, atelier
- **Palette families:** mono (sample: Graphite)

**Use when**
- Portraits, fashion, architecture, memorial and documentary subjects
- A photograph with real light and contrast
- Timelessness is wanted

**Avoid when**
- Food and colour-dependent products
- Weak or flat photographs
- Festive and children's subjects

**Pitfalls**
- Grey, flat conversions
- Type placed over busy tone
- Using it to hide a poor colour photograph

<a id="gradient-futurism"></a>
## Gradient Futurism

Screen-native light: glows, soft meshes and glass for products that live in software.

**Principles**
- Light as the material: glow, blur, depth
- One dominant hue shift
- Crisp type over soft colour
- Dark stages for luminous objects

- **Grid:** Simple central or left-aligned layout; the gradient supplies the drama.
- **Typography:** Clean sans in two weights; large, tight headlines.
- **Colour:** A two or three stop gradient from palette roles only; never rainbow.
- **Imagery:** render, none, illustration
- **Compositions:** Typographic poster; Rendered stage; Product hero
- **Pairings we ship:** modern, swiss, signal, artline
- **Palette families:** cool, bold (sample: Deep Sea)

**Use when**
- Software, fintech, AI products, conferences, app launches
- Screen-first formats
- A brand with no physical product to photograph

**Avoid when**
- Print on uncoated paper, where gradients band
- Food, craft and heritage
- Brands already using it everywhere: it is the default look of the sector

**Pitfalls**
- Purple-to-blue sameness
- Low contrast text on mid-tones
- Gradient as a substitute for an idea

<a id="collage"></a>
## Collage

Cut and paste since Dada: unrelated pictures joined to make a new meaning.

**Principles**
- Juxtaposition creates the idea
- Visible cut edges
- Scale shifts between elements
- A limited paper palette holds it together

- **Grid:** Free arrangement around one anchor figure.
- **Typography:** Cut-out or plain; labels as part of the composition.
- **Colour:** Paper tones with two or three flat colours.
- **Imagery:** collage, cut-out, duotone
- **Compositions:** Layered collage; Framed portrait; Typographic poster
- **Pairings we ship:** editorial, street, gallery, masthead, studio
- **Palette families:** warm, earth, mono (sample: Saffron Market)

**Use when**
- Culture, fashion, music, editorial covers, campaigns with a concept
- Several good cut-out images exist
- Wit is welcome

**Avoid when**
- Only one image exists
- Regulated sectors where juxtaposition could imply a claim
- Tiny formats

**Pitfalls**
- Images with no licence to be cut and combined
- No anchor: a pile, not a picture
- Combining people in ways that imply endorsement

<a id="typographic-monumentalism"></a>
## Typographic Monumentalism

The word as a building: letters at architectural scale, cropped by the frame.

**Principles**
- One word or number fills the surface
- Cropping is deliberate
- Detail text is tiny by contrast
- Flat colour, no effects

- **Grid:** Letters define the grid; details align to stems and counters.
- **Typography:** One very heavy or very condensed face; tight tracking.
- **Colour:** Two colours.
- **Imagery:** none, cut-out
- **Compositions:** Monumental type; Typographic poster; Band and field
- **Pairings we ship:** statement, impact, poster, broadcast, works
- **Palette families:** bold, mono, warm (sample: Royal Orange)

**Use when**
- Openings, sales, dates, anniversaries, billboards
- There is no image, and one word says it
- Read at distance or at speed

**Avoid when**
- The key word is long or weak
- Several facts of equal weight
- Quiet premium brands

**Pitfalls**
- Cropping that makes the word unreadable
- Detail text too small for the format
- A weak word made large

## Composition archetypes

The generators are built in CI-3. Three concepts for one brief must use three different archetypes.

| Archetype | Idea | Leads with | Needs a picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Image to the edges | One photograph fills the surface and the words sit in its quiet area. | image-led | yes |
| Typographic poster | The words are the picture: one statement set large on a field of colour. | headline-led | no |
| Editorial split | An uneven two-part page, picture on one side and reading on the other. | image-led | yes |
| Modular grid | A visible column grid holds every element; alignment is the ornament. | information-led | no |
| Mounted frame | A picture mounted inside a wide margin, like a print in a mount. | image-led | yes |
| Monumental type | Letters so large they crop at the edges and become architecture. | headline-led | no |
| Product hero | One object, cut out or staged, on a plain field with a short line. | image-led | yes |
| Layered collage | Cut pictures, paper and type overlapped with deliberate tension. | image-led | yes |
| Information ledger | Rows and columns of facts with rules between them: a timetable or price list made handsome. | information-led | no |
| Centred emblem | A symmetrical stack around a mark or name, with generous quiet space. | headline-led | no |
| Band and field | A strong band carries identity; the field below carries the message. | information-led | no |
| Diagonal energy | Type and shapes set on an angle so the surface moves. | headline-led | no |
| Framed portrait | A person in a shaped window, with name and role beside it. | image-led | yes |
| Pattern field | A repeat pattern carries the surface and a calm panel carries the words. | headline-led | no |
| Rendered stage | A lit three-dimensional object or scene with type in the same light. | image-led | yes |
| Serial tiles | One system repeated across several tiles, each a variation. | information-led | no |
